### 0. Verdict

- **The rigorous instrument measures the wrong thing.** `run_bench.py` and `htb_rescore.py` score autonomous solves, which the owner's PASS criteria explicitly do not require. Nothing in `tests/`, `benchmark/` or `scripts/` scores right-method, offset, decompile content or template completability. The tool can be "5/7 solved" by the Sept docs and roughly 0/7 on PASS (per the brief's re-measurement) at the same time.
- **The seven HTB targets are now a training set.** At least four executors were written from a single target each, HTB names appear 35 times in 11 files under `supwngo/`, and the variant corpora were authored after reversing the target. They are regression suites, not generalisation evidence.
- **There is no held-out number.** R3/R4/R5 were authored, never cold-measured, then deleted from the repo; R2 was developed against after its cold run.
- **The team finds its own measurement errors well, but only after decisions have been built on them.** The 3/7 legacy figure launched a port effort; the "OK cells" matrix scores JSON parseability; the HTB rescore credits a self-reported shell flag.
- **Highest-leverage move:** build a PASS scorecard driven by a ground-truth manifest and a randomised synthetic generator (ELF and PE from the same sources, offset truth from DWARF), run nightly on a self-hosted x86-64 runner, with a held-out seed the developing agents never see. Until then every sprint exit criterion is an anecdote.

### 2. Recommendations

**D5-R1 — PASS scorecard and ground-truth manifest.**
- Add `benchmark/passcard.py` and a per-target `truth.yaml` with: `format`, `arch`, `vuln_class` (CWE), `methods_accept[]`, `vuln_function`, `primitive` (stack: `buf_to_ret`, `buf_to_canary`; heap: chunk size and reach), `win_target`, `input_vector`, and `decompile_anchors` (function to expected callees or constants).
- Metrics, each PASS / FAIL / ERROR per target with reps:
  - **M1** method listed; **M2** method recommended (top-1), with a penalty for confident wrong answers.
  - **M3** offset exact, labelled static or dynamic.
  - **M3b** RIP-control proof: a payload built from the reported offset crashes at a chosen PC, witnessed by gdb or core dump (the CGC "Type-1 PoV" idea).
  - **M4** decompile: fraction of truth functions with non-placeholder pseudo-C containing their anchors, plus backend and seconds.
  - **M5** criterion (a): rerun with truth-supplied `--winfun/--eip/--offset`, then verify the flag under the existing `benchmark/attribution.py`.
  - **M6** criterion (b): the template must declare machine-readable placeholders (for example `# SUPWNGO-INPUT name:type`); the harness fills them from `truth.yaml` and runs under attribution.
  - **M7** no traceback, timeout or bad JSON; **M8** wall time.
- Fix `htb_rescore.py` to gate on `shell_proven`, and retire "OK cells" as a KPI.
- **Impact:** makes the owner's bar a number. **Effort:** S for the harness and seven truth files; M with M5/M6. **Dependencies:** `--winfun/--eip` flags and template placeholders (CLI and engine dimensions). **Risk:** `methods_accept` labels are a judgement call; keep them reviewed and versioned.
- **Success measure:** a committed baseline on the 7 HTB targets where every metric has at least one FAIL (proof it can fail), and a deliberate mutant (force `template` to ret2system) flips M2 and M6 on at least 5 of 7.

**D5-R2 — Synthetic generator with automatic truth.**
- Add `benchmark/gen/`: C templates per class (stack BOF to ret2win / ret2libc / SROP / shellcode, fmtstr, integer truncation, off-by-one, UAF, heap overflow, custom allocator) crossed with a seeded sampler.
- Sample over: buffer sizes, local ordering, win-function names drawn from a wordlist disjoint from `WIN_FUNCTIONS`, input API, menu depth, protections, static or dynamic linking, stripping, compiler (gcc 9/11/13, clang) and `-O0/-O1/-O2`.
- Extract truth mechanically: build with `-g`, read frame offsets from DWARF with pyelftools, then strip. Derive the method label from a template × protections decision table. Read the flag from `flag.txt` (R4/R8 of `benchmark/README.md`).
- Build the same sources with mingw x64/x86 for PE decompile and offset truth.
- Include metamorphic pairs (rename symbols, strip, change compiler); method and offset must be invariant.
- **Impact:** an unlimited, uncontaminated, licence-free corpus; the direct cure for F2. **Effort:** M. **Dependencies:** R1. **Risk:** template monoculture; mitigate with at least three structurally different templates per class and public anchors (R4).
- **Success measure:** 300 dev-seed binaries (at least 60 PE) build with 100% truth extraction, and the reference solver, fed truth, reaches the flag on at least 95% of ELF positives (proves the truth is right).

**D5-R3 — Restore held-out discipline.**
- Move R3/R4/R5 from `/srv/share/dev/_archive/…` to a location no development agent's worktree can read. Cold-measure R3 now under R1 plus solve, pre-registered in the R2 report's format.
- Rules:
  - A measured set is burnt and becomes dev.
  - Any target whose slug appears under `supwngo/` is training (lint: slug grep must return 0 for held-out; today the HTB set returns 35).
  - The generator's held-out seed lives with the owner; the harness returns aggregates only.
  - Rotate quarterly.
- **Impact:** the only honest generalisation number. **Effort:** S. **Dependencies:** R1. **Risk:** agents with filesystem access are the contamination channel; custody must be physical or permissioned.
- **Success measure:** an R3 cold report exists with a dev-minus-held-out gap per metric, and the gap shrinks quarter on quarter.

**D5-R4 — Tiered corpus with stated licence handling.**

| Tier | Source | Use | Redistribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | R2 generator | dev and held-out seeds | own, free |
| T1 | ROP Emporium, how2heap built against pinned glibcs, picoCTF archives, the "Nightmare" technique-sorted set | method-ID and solve, dev only | fetch-by-URL plus sha256, do not vendor (licences UNVERIFIED) |
| T2 | Trail of Bits `cb-multios` (DARPA CGC with PoVs and patched twins), NIST Juliet C/C++ good/bad pairs for CWE-121/122/134/190/415/416 | detector precision and recall | open; verify licences before vendoring |
| T3 | HTB, pwn.college | smoke set, never the headline | local only |
| PE | R2 mingw builds; a few open-source Windows binaries shipped with PDBs | decompile coverage, offset | own or upstream licence |

- Contamination rule for LLM hooks: public CTF binaries and write-ups are in model training data. LLM-assisted scores count only on T0-fresh or post-cutoff sets, and are always reported separately from deterministic scores.
- **Effort:** M. **Dependencies:** R1. **Risk:** label cost for T1; start with 40 targets.
- **Success measure:** `report` precision and recall per CWE on at least 500 Juliet pairs, and M1/M2 on at least 40 T1 binaries, both in the nightly table.

**D5-R5 — Self-hosted runner with three lanes and ratchets.**
- To my knowledge DGX Spark (GB10) is ARM64, so x86-64 targets cannot run natively there. The corpus runner must be an x86-64 host; the Sparks serve models only. Please confirm (Q2).
- Use a Forgejo/Gitea `act_runner` (reuses the existing workflow syntax, works air-gapped) or plain systemd timers calling `scripts/ci/gate.sh`.
- Lanes:
  - **Pre-merge**, at most 10 minutes: `-m "not slow"` with xdist, no `-x`, plus passcard on a 30-binary T0 slice.
  - **Nightly:** full suite three times (flake census), all dev tiers, variant families rebuilt at a second compiler and `-O` level.
  - **Weekly:** a tool-version-bump canary image and the held-out aggregate.
- Gate: commit `benchmark/baselines/passcard.json`; any per-target PASS-to-FAIL flip blocks the merge unless listed in the PR. Append summaries to a committed `benchmark/history/*.jsonl`.
- Add `SUPWNGO_REQUIRE_TARGETS=1` so that on the runner a skip for a missing fixture is a failure, with a skip allowlist.
- **Effort:** M. **Dependencies:** R1, R6. **Risk:** nightly cost; cap ladder time per target.
- **Success measure:** 14 consecutive nightlies with an identical skip set, and a flake rate below 1 per 3 full runs or the test is quarantined by name.

**D5-R6 — Pinned environment and tool manifest.**

### 3. Hypotheses and experiments

- **D5-H1.** On the 7 HTB targets, recommended-method (M2) is at most 2/7 while the documented solve rate is 5/7. *Experiment:* hand-label truth and run R1. *Falsifier:* M2 is at least 5/7.
- **D5-H2.** ret2win identification is name-driven. *Experiment:* rename win functions in 20 T0 or R1 binaries to random words and compare M1. *Falsifier:* the drop is under 10 percentage points.
- **D5-H3.** The family executors are compiler-fitted. *Experiment:* rebuild five variant families at `-O2` and with clang, then run `measure_family.py`. *Falsifier:* positives solved fall by less than 10%.
- **D5-H4.** The held-out gap is large. *Experiment:* run R3 cold. *Falsifier:* R3 solves at 5/5 reliability are at least 80% of the count.
- **D5-H5.** Static offset inference (Ghidra or DWARF-validated frame analysis) beats cyclic crashing. *Experiment:* run both on 100 T0 stack targets, including menu-driven, canaried and PE ones. *Falsifier:* static exact-match is below 90%, or dynamic is within 10 points of it.
- **D5-H6.** The "load flake" is contention, not nondeterminism. *Experiment:* 10 serial full runs on an idle host. *Falsifier:* any failure.
- **D5-H7.** The current `Dockerfile` cannot reproduce the corpus results. *Experiment:* build it, mount the repo and run `corpus_r2`. *Falsifier:* 15/15.
- **D5-H8.** Review rounds do not predict fewer post-merge errata. *Experiment:* count errata and "pre-existing failure" entries per merged unit from the docs, split by reviewed versus corpus-first. *Falsifier:* reviewed units show at most half the rate.
- **D5-H9.** HTB is useless for scoring LLM hooks. *Experiment:* give a local model only each challenge's name and ask for the technique. *Falsifier:* at most 1/7 correct.
- **D5-H10.** Echo-ambiguous shells inflate the HTB score. *Experiment:* re-score gating on `shell_proven` under strace attribution. *Falsifier:* the same solved set.

### 4. Best-in-class gap

- **DARPA CGC (Mayhem, Mechanical Phish, Xandra).** An independent referee scores PoVs with typed proofs: Type 1 is register control, Type 2 is memory disclosure. *Copy:* graded proof levels (M3b) and paired vulnerable/patched binaries via `cb-multios`. *Skip:* patching and availability scoring.
- **AIxCC.** Hidden vulnerabilities and an accuracy multiplier that penalises wrong submissions. *Copy:* the penalty for confident wrong recommendations; `pwn` currently pays nothing for recommending ROP_EXECVE on a ret2win. *Skip:* patch scoring and scale infrastructure.
- **FuzzBench / OSS-Fuzz.** Many trials, statistical tests and automatic reports per commit. *Copy:* reps (already present), paired per-target comparisons and committed history. *Skip:* cluster orchestration.
- **Magma, LAVA-M, Juliet.** Ground truth by construction. *Copy:* Juliet pairs for detector precision and recall. *Skip:* LAVA-style injected bugs, which are unrealistic for method-ID.
- **pwn.college.** Per-user randomised challenge instances; that is R2. Copy the idea, not the content.
- **Cybench, NYU CTF Bench, CyberSecEval.** Subtask-guided scoring gives partial credit when the solver is handed a component, which is exactly the owner's (a)/(b). *Copy:* that structure and their contamination caveats. *Skip:* treating public CTF solve rate as a headline for LLM modes.
- **SWE-bench Verified / Live.** Human-validated subsets and rolling fresh sets. *Copy:* quarterly rotation.
- **Decompiler evaluation (Dogbolt-style cross-decompiler comparison, re-executability metrics in neural-decompilation work).** *Copy:* anchor-recall and cross-backend agreement. *Skip:* recompilability, which is too costly for the stated bar.
- **angr and pwntools.** A separate, pinned binaries repository exercised in CI. *Copy:* a fixtures repo pinned by commit and exercised nightly.

### 5. Questions for the owner

1. Is the product an analyst-assist tool (the PASS criteria) or an autonomous solver? They pull the roadmap in different directions; if PASS is the bar, executor breadth stops being the priority.
2. Is there an x86-64 host for the runner? If the fleet is only DGX Sparks, everything target-executing needs emulation, which changes exploit behaviour.
3. Who is custodian of held-out sets and seeds, and will you accept that development agents can never read them?
4. Is "right method" top-1 or top-k, and who signs off `methods_accept` per target?
5. What does "offset on all" mean for non-stack bugs (auth-or-out, sabotage, bon-nie-appetit)? I recommend redefining it as "primitive location and parameters"; otherwise the metric is undefined on 3/7.
6. For PE, are mingw fixtures enough, or must MSVC-built binaries pass? Real PE is mostly MSVC; mingw overstates readiness.
7. Are LLM-assisted results reported separately from deterministic ones, and is the offline fleet the only configuration that counts?
8. Who consumes releases? If it is only the VR platform's agents, "nightly-green tag" can replace versioned releases.
9. Will the Docker image be distributed? That determines how much licence diligence the bundled tools and fetched corpora need under PolyForm Strict.
