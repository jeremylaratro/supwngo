# Dimension D5 — Evaluation, benchmarks, ecosystem and process (adversarial)

Your dimension: how will the owner *know* supwngo is getting better — and is the current way of
measuring honest? Scope:
- **Benchmark design.** Seven HTB ELF targets is tiny and overfittable. Propose a tiered corpus:
  synthetic generated targets with ground truth (a generator per vuln class, randomised layouts), public
  training sets (ROP Emporium, pwn.college, how2heap, picoCTF / other CTF archives, DARPA CGC
  binaries, Juliet-style suites), held-out sets, and PE fixtures. Address contamination, held-out
  discipline, and licence/redistribution limits.
- **Metrics.** Method-identification accuracy, offset accuracy, decompile coverage, detection
  precision/recall, attributed solve rate (shell or planted flag reproduced, not self-reported
  success), time-to-result, robustness (no crash/timeout). Map them to the owner's PASS criteria.
  Point out where existing reports in `docs/reports/` and `benchmark/` measured the wrong thing or
  could not fail (see the erratum in `docs/reports/2026-09-26-comprehensive-feature-retest.md`).
- **Test and release infrastructure without CI.** Local pytest only today (~2,230 tests, 92 files,
  a known load flake). Propose a local or self-hosted runner (e.g. on the owner's GPU/CPU nodes),
  nightly corpus runs, regression gates, reproducible environments (`Dockerfile` exists), and a
  release cadence. Read `tests/`, `benchmark/`, `scripts/`, `Dockerfile`, `CHANGELOG.md`.
- **Tooling ecosystem.** Which external tools should be pinned and integrated for a reproducible
  environment (pwndbg/GEF, one_gadget, seccomp-tools, patchelf/pwninit, libc databases, ROPgadget,
  wine for PE, Ghidra/PyGhidra, rizin, Qiling, fuzzers) and how to keep them versioned.
- **Process.** The repo's sprint/plan/review process (`docs/plans/`, `docs/process/`): is it producing
  capability, or documents? Where is effort being wasted?
Use `D5` as the ID prefix.
